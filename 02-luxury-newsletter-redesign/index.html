Of tea, soup, castle and fires.
Happy New Year, Friend
I hope you had a wonderful break and a gentle start to the new year. I spent my break with my in-laws in the UK and then celebrated my birthday (I'm 43, which is awesome as it's a prime number!) and New Year's Eve in one of my favourite places: the Chateau Coliving in Normandy, France. Yep, I returned for the 3rd time in one year, there's just something magical about this place, even (or especially in the depth of winter). Despite being sick with a nasty cold (and losing my voice after singing karaoke until 4 am on NYE 😂), it's been the perfect place to start the New Year, with lovely people, cosy fires, fun board games and lots and lots of tea. Of course, the world, big brands and the ever-powerful algorithm have been sending me the not-so-subtle message that it's time to hustle! To jump straight back into work, to hit the ground running...and all the usual nonsense. 🙄 In the past, I got sucked into this so quickly, I'd have a tummy ache from anxiety before the first week of the year was even over. 🥺
Thankfully, I've been working very hard on questioning this narrative, both personally as well as within DNG and our membership, The Lab (which just entered its 9th year, yaaaay!). And I'm definitely noticing a big shift in New Year's vibe, as many of us have been shedding layer upon layer of ingrained hustle culture and toxic productivity. We started the year in the Lab with our usual coworking sessions as well as some gentle, cosy events: Craft & Chat and Tea Time. January's Tea Time topic was Easing Into The Year and we all shared how we're arriving in this new year and what easing in means to us. I thought I'd share a few ideas and thoughts with you today:
The Winter Library
Easing Into the Year
CASTLE MAINTENANCE
Here at the Chateau, one of the most important maintenance activities is keeping the firewood chopped and stocked, so we can keep the cosy fire going all day. It's a slow, manual and very mindful task, and surprisingly enjoyable. 🪵 Maintenance might feel less exciting than starting work on a shiny new idea, but it's just as important, if not more. Who knows, it might even save you a bunch of work in the future.
What are some maintenance tasks you might like to dedicate some time to this winter? I'd love to hear about them! Hit reply!
A TIME TO SHRAPEN OUR TOOLS
Our member Laura P. shared that January is a time for composting the old to allow new things to grow. It's a time to sharpen your tools 🪚, check the pantry stocks 🍯, or mend what's broken 🪡.I absolutely love this imagery, and it's a wonderful reminder that this time of year is great for maintenance, not so much for growth or exploration.
